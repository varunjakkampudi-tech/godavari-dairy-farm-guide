Feed, water and fodder planning

A buffalo ration is a combination of roughage, concentrate and mineral sources balanced for animal weight, milk yield/composition, pregnancy, body condition and available feeds. No one fixed ration is safe or efficient for every animal. NDDB offers animal-specific ration balancing; discuss formulation with a trained nutritionist or veterinarian.

Evidence and safety: General guidance only. Confirm plot, buyer, legal and veterinary decisions with current local authorities and qualified professionals. Unverified regional details remain explicitly marked.

Why it matters

Feed clean water freely and check trough function and cleanliness daily. Record feed offered and refusals. Introduce ration changes gradually. Keep mouldy, rotten, chemically contaminated or unknown plants away. Store concentrates dry and protected from rodents and moisture.

Practical guidance

Feed groups: green fodder supplies water and nutrients; dry roughage supports rumen function; concentrate provides denser nutrients; mineral mixture and salt address micronutrient needs. Exact quantities depend on dry matter, analysis and production. NDDB examples for a 500 kg buffalo are illustrative examples, not prescriptions for an unknown animal.

Use this safely

One leased acre must also fit shed, yard, access, manure and water infrastructure. Therefore calculate the net cultivable area rather than assuming the whole acre grows fodder. Compare season-by-season green fodder yield and irrigation reliability against herd demand; maintain a purchased fodder/silage contingency and reserve storage.

Local application

For each crop, verify locally recommended variety, planting windows, seed/cutting source, irrigation, harvest interval, expected yield and disease risks with the agriculture/horticulture office. Hybrid Napier, fodder maize and sorghum can be candidates, but local suitability and water availability must be checked before planting.
