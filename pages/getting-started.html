Why it matters

Before any deposit: get written buyer terms; inspect the exact plot in dry and rainy conditions; verify water year-round; request permission to build from the lessor and relevant local authority; get dated cost quotes; find a veterinarian; and calculate an annual downside case.

Practical guidance

Do not use a single peak milk yield for twelve months. Model each animal’s lactation and dry period, stagger likely calvings, allow for illness and breeding delay, and include replacement and mortality risk. Fixed employee and lease costs continue even when milk sales fall.

Use this safely

A one-acre lease may be useful for housing and some fodder but does not guarantee enough feed. Compare purchased-feed logistics and water with on-site growing. Use a written lease term long enough to recover only those improvements the lessor has authorized.

Local application

Proceed only when a conservative annual model is financeable, buyer terms are documented, animal inspection is arranged, a leave/absence plan exists and lease permissions are written. Otherwise pause, redesign, or do not invest.
