Vaccination and health records

Vaccination plans are disease-, age-, location- and programme-dependent. The local government veterinarian should enter the current schedule for each identified animal. Government campaign rounds and vaccine availability can change.

Evidence and safety: General guidance only. Confirm plot, buyer, legal and veterinary decisions with current local authorities and qualified professionals. Unverified regional details remain explicitly marked.

Build an animal-specific plan with your veterinarian

There is no universal schedule suitable for every age, pregnancy stage, previous dose, disease exposure or current campaign. Ask the local veterinarian to confirm the product, animal eligibility, timing, boosters, campaign status, cold-chain, contraindications, administering professional and next review date. The examples below are questions, not directions to vaccinate.

DAHD programme materials and NDDB reference schedules explain national disease-control context. Campaign implementation, availability and animal-specific timing must be checked locally; do not treat an online schedule as a prescription. Brucellosis is zoonotic: avoid unprotected contact with abortion materials and seek public-health advice after exposure.

Open farm records to enter a vet-confirmed health event

In the health module, record date, animal ID, description, status, follow-up and any withdrawal end date supplied by the veterinarian or product label. The register supports editing/search/backup but never calculates vaccine due dates or withdrawal periods.

No product list, dose, self-medication instruction or automated withdrawal calculator is provided. Follow the written veterinary instruction and current product label; when uncertain, keep milk separate and ask the veterinarian and buyer.

Why it matters

Ask the local veterinarian to confirm eligible animals, vaccine product, booster/recall interval, season or campaign window, pregnancy considerations, cold-chain handling and documentation. Ask which diseases are locally indicated; do not copy a generic online calendar.

Practical guidance

Common bovine programme topics include foot-and-mouth disease and brucellosis control. Hemorrhagic septicemia and black quarter recommendations depend on local risk and state veterinary advice. Other vaccines must not be added automatically.

Use this safely

Record animal ID, disease/vaccine, batch/expiry, administration date, administering professional, next due date as instructed, adverse event and source. Keep invoice/campaign slip. Make a due list but require veterinarian confirmation before administering.

Local application

Never give an injection based on this guide. Do not use expired or temperature-abused vaccine; preserve cold-chain as directed by the official/vaccine label and professional.
