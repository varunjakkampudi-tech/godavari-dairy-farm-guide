01 — Before paying any advance

No buffalo purchase, land deposit, or permanent construction until the buyer, land, veterinary, and cash-flow gates below are documented.

02 — Buffalo purchase inspection

Use a separate sheet for every proposed animal. An owner should accompany the independent veterinarian.

03 — Everyday workflow (illustrative times)

Times must be adjusted to actual buyer pickup, climate and labor availability. Every animal has its own feed, milk, and health entry.

04 — Weather operating switches

Change preventive routines when conditions change; never rely on a calendar month alone.

05 — Health escalation rules

Employees observe and report; they do not diagnose, inject or administer prescription medicines without veterinary direction.

06 — First 90 days of business

Treat the first three months as a measured pilot, not a promise of profit.

07 — 30-day, 90-day, six-month and first-year decision gates

Use this as a dated evidence review, not a forecast. Enter measured values from your own records; there are no default profit, yield or expansion targets because local price, land, animal and cost evidence is not verified. The decision owner records GO, HOLD or STOP with the evidence and reason. A blank, unsupported or unresolved material item means HOLD. Any immediate animal-welfare, food-safety, legal, water, site or cash-safety failure means STOP until corrected.

Day 30 — ready before purchase

Measure and attach: dated buyer settlement examples and written acceptance/payment terms; surveyed usable land and flood/drainage notes; written lease permissions and term; measured water availability and source; dated itemized quotes for animals, feed, labor, works, transport, vet and insurance; low/base/stress cash plans and available working capital; named primary/backup veterinary access.

GO only if every critical item has a dated source, responsibilities and costs fit the written lease, water/drainage and access are dependable, the buyer confirms an acceptable milk/settlement path, and downside cash requirements are funded. A qualified local vet and site professional have reviewed the relevant animal and construction plans. Otherwise HOLD; unsafe or legally unauthorized site conditions mean STOP.

Day 90 — controlled pilot

Measure from day one: animal-level morning/evening milk and saleable volume; rejected/withheld milk; buyer tests, deductions and payment dates; feed offered/refused and dated spend; labor hours/relief coverage; water/power interruptions; health events, veterinary spend and treatments; actual transport, cleaning and other operating costs.

GO to normal operation only if records reconcile to buyer settlements and receipts, milk handling and worker coverage are safe, unresolved health/withdrawal issues are zero, and actual cash performance has been recalculated against the owner’s conservative survival requirement. Do not expand at day 90 solely because output is temporarily high. Repeated unexplained losses, unsafe care or missed essential payments mean HOLD/STOP and a corrective review.

Month 6 — season and resilience

Compare actuals with the plan: each month’s saleable milk, realized ₹ per saleable unit, feed cost and availability/gaps, labor and relief hours, health/reproduction/dry status, rejected milk, utility interruptions, cash balance and due obligations. Document which seasonal conditions have actually occurred; do not infer a full-year result from six months.

GO to continue at current scale only if seasonal feed and water contingencies are documented, the buyer’s actual settlements match the model inputs, recurring operating obligations are covered from observed receipts plus a documented reserve, and a local veterinarian reviews health/reproduction concerns. HOLD if a season or material cost remains unobserved; STOP for unresolved welfare, safety, legal, or inability-to-pay essentials.

Month 12 — complete annual cycle

Reconcile twelve months: actual capital and operating costs, owner and hired labor, saleable milk and settlement by buyer, dry periods/reproduction, veterinary and replacement events, feed seasonality and losses, lease/utility obligations, debt service, cash low point and remaining reserve. Separate cash operating margin from depreciation, financing, tax and owner labor; obtain accountant review where relevant.

Expansion GO requires a complete reconciled annual dataset, all major seasons observed, written buyer capacity/terms for additional milk, sufficient tested site/feed capacity, professional veterinary and engineering input as applicable, and downside cash needs funded without relying on unverified prices or yield. If any is missing, HOLD at current safe scale and collect evidence. A failed reserve, unsafe site/animals or unresolved legal permission means STOP expansion and correct the cause.

Evidence file index: keep dated, privacy-safe IDs for receipts, settlement slips, test results, measurements, vet advice, quotes and decisions. Do not treat these browser fields as a backup or as proof of independent verification.

Use finance studio, buyer settlement comparison, monthly fodder balance, farm registers and conceptual layout to collect and compare the inputs. Site and business suitability still require local fieldwork.

Field verification: collect real evidence

Use the template below for calls or visits. Avoid entering private personal information on shared devices. Save notes separately: this worksheet intentionally does not upload or automatically save your notes.

Milk buyer questions

  1. Which nearby village route and pickup time?
  2. Will you accept buffalo milk from a new farmer with 3–4 animals?
  3. What is the fat/SNF testing procedure and settlement formula?
  4. Are prices quoted per litre, kilogram or kilogram of fat?
  5. What are the deductions, rejection criteria and payment delay?
  6. Can you provide a dated, anonymized settlement example?

Landowner / plot questions

  1. Written lease term and rent escalation?
  2. Livestock, shed, pump, fencing and wastewater permissions?
  3. Monsoon waterlogging and flood history?
  4. Water source, electricity and accessible road?
  5. Who owns/removes improvements at lease end?
  6. Can a vet, buyer vehicle and emergency transport reach the site?

Livestock seller questions

  1. Identity, lactation number and last calving date?
  2. Morning/evening witnessed milkings over multiple days?
  3. Reproductive and medication history?
  4. Independent veterinarian examination permitted?
  5. Transport/insurance documents and quarantine plan?
  6. What happens if documented representations are false?

Keep evidence: dated photos of milk tests, written terms, receipts, plot dimensions and veterinary advice. Record the source and distinguish a seller claim from independent verification.

Stop / review triggers

  • No confirmed buyer or no written milk payment formula.
  • Employee wages and feed cannot be covered during low-production or dry periods.
  • Landowner will not authorize livestock infrastructure in writing.
  • No dependable clean water, drainage or veterinary access.
  • Buffalo seller refuses independent examination or actual milking observation.
  • Ill animal, unsafe milk handling, or unresolved withdrawal period: do not release affected milk.
