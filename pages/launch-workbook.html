Farm launch & employee workbook

A practical sequence for a proposed 3–4-buffalo farm on one leased acre near Bhimavaram, Tanuku or Palakollu. Check every step against your real buyer, plot and veterinarian. Times and examples are adaptable, not a substitute for qualified advice.

Not a veterinary prescription or investment approval. No local buyer pricing, farmer profitability, animal health or plot conditions have been verified for your farm. Do not use the checkboxes alone to certify readiness.

01 — Before paying any advance

No buffalo purchase, land deposit, or permanent construction until the buyer, land, veterinary, and cash-flow gates below are documented.

02 — Buffalo purchase inspection

Use a separate sheet for every proposed animal. An owner should accompany the independent veterinarian.

03 — Everyday workflow (illustrative times)

Times must be adjusted to actual buyer pickup, climate and labor availability. Every animal has its own feed, milk, and health entry.

04 — Weather operating switches

Change preventive routines when conditions change; never rely on a calendar month alone.

05 — Health escalation rules

Employees observe and report; they do not diagnose, inject or administer prescription medicines without veterinary direction.

06 — First 90 days of business

Treat the first three months as a measured pilot, not a promise of profit.

Field verification: collect real evidence

Use the template below for calls or visits. Avoid entering private personal information on shared devices. Save notes separately: this worksheet intentionally does not upload or automatically save your notes.

Milk buyer questions

  1. Which nearby village route and pickup time?
  2. Will you accept buffalo milk from a new farmer with 3–4 animals?
  3. What is the fat/SNF testing procedure and settlement formula?
  4. Are prices quoted per litre, kilogram or kilogram of fat?
  5. What are the deductions, rejection criteria and payment delay?
  6. Can you provide a dated, anonymized settlement example?

Landowner / plot questions

  1. Written lease term and rent escalation?
  2. Livestock, shed, pump, fencing and wastewater permissions?
  3. Monsoon waterlogging and flood history?
  4. Water source, electricity and accessible road?
  5. Who owns/removes improvements at lease end?
  6. Can a vet, buyer vehicle and emergency transport reach the site?

Livestock seller questions

  1. Identity, lactation number and last calving date?
  2. Morning/evening witnessed milkings over multiple days?
  3. Reproductive and medication history?
  4. Independent veterinarian examination permitted?
  5. Transport/insurance documents and quarantine plan?
  6. What happens if documented representations are false?

Keep evidence: dated photos of milk tests, written terms, receipts, plot dimensions and veterinary advice. Record the source and distinguish a seller claim from independent verification.

Stop / review triggers

  • No confirmed buyer or no written milk payment formula.
  • Employee wages and feed cannot be covered during low-production or dry periods.
  • Landowner will not authorize livestock infrastructure in writing.
  • No dependable clean water, drainage or veterinary access.
  • Buffalo seller refuses independent examination or actual milking observation.
  • Ill animal, unsafe milk handling, or unresolved withdrawal period: do not release affected milk.
