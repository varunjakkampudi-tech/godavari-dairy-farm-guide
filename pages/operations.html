Daily farm operations

A single trained worker may handle a very small herd when routines are designed around safe handling and the owner provides leave coverage. It is not a robust seven-day staffing plan by itself. Milking, cleaning, fodder handling and emergencies create early/late work; confirm lawful wages, rest, safety and relief coverage.

Why it matters

Example routine (adjust with buyer collection times): 05:00 animal check and water; 05:15 clean and prepare; 05:45 milking and yield records; 06:30 feed; 07:00 dung removal and wash equipment; 10:00 water, heat and appetite check; 13:00 feed/water and shed inspection; 16:00 prepare; 16:30 evening milking; 17:15 feed and equipment sanitation; 20:00 final water, security and animal check.

Practical guidance

Daily record: animal ID, appetite, rumination/behavior, water, dung/urine observations, gait, udder/milk changes, feed offered/refused, morning/evening milk, treatment/vet contact and unresolved actions. Escalate unusual signs promptly; do not medicate on a checklist.

Use this safely

Weekly: inspect troughs, drains, fencing, roof leaks, fly breeding sites, feed stores and milking equipment; review milk and feed totals. Monthly: review body condition, reproductive records, expenses, inventory, worker training and equipment maintenance with appropriate professional help.

Relief plan — complete and post locally before leave or absence

  1. Name a primary trained relief person and a second backup; agree availability, fair shift/rest coverage and lawful terms with the owner.
  2. Show both people the animal IDs, water/feed routine, safe entry/handling, two milking/handoff times, sanitation, milk segregation and where records are kept.
  3. Post owner and verified veterinarian contacts, transport/access instructions, and the rule: observe, keep safe, call the owner/vet; never diagnose, inject or use prescription medicines.
  4. At each handover, pass unresolved signs, professional instructions exactly as given, milk withdrawal status, feed/water stock, equipment issues and who accepted follow-up.
  5. If trained cover is unavailable for essential care, the owner must arrange safe alternate care before leaving; do not leave animals unattended or treat a single worker as seven-day coverage.

Local application

Worker safety: use calm animal handling, avoid blind spots and crushing zones, wear suitable footwear and gloves, use mechanical help for heavy loads, keep first-aid and emergency contacts accessible, and train backup labor. Never leave one worker solely responsible for unattended emergency treatment.
