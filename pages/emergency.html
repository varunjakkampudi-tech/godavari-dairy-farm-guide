Emergency veterinary contacts

Use these official district references to locate veterinary help. Listed office numbers are not advertised as 24-hour emergency hotlines; call to confirm the current on-call service and the nearest facility for your village.

Immediate danger? Breathing distress, collapse, inability to rise, severe bloat, suspected poisoning, difficult calving, or major bleeding needs urgent veterinary attention. Ask the District Animal Husbandry Office to route you if you do not already have the local veterinarian’s number.

Village / mandal veterinarian

The district department describes veterinary hospitals, dispensaries, rural livestock units, disease diagnostic labs and mobile ambulatory veterinary clinics. Facility staffing and contact schedules can change.

Ask the district office or nearest veterinary institution to confirm your mandal contact and on-call coverage. Save it offline before an emergency.

What to tell the veterinarian

  1. Your village, exact location and a callback number.
  2. Animal ID, age/approximate weight, pregnancy or calving status.
  3. What changed, when it began, and whether it is getting worse.
  4. Appetite, water intake, breathing, dung, milk, walking and temperature only if measured safely by a trained person.
  5. Recent calving, travel, feed change, injury, exposure, medicines or vaccination.
  6. Whether other animals are sick and whether infectious disease is suspected.

Keep treated/abnormal milk separate as directed. Do not force fluids into a weak animal, give human or leftover medicines, or attempt injections/IVs. Restrict movement if contagious disease is possible and follow the veterinarian’s directions.
