Flies, ticks, mosquitoes and biosecurity

Reduce breeding conditions first: remove wet manure and spoiled feed, drain standing water, keep bedding dry, clean troughs and manage manure. Identify and isolate incoming or sick animals as directed by a veterinarian.

Evidence and safety: General guidance only. Confirm plot, buyer, legal and veterinary decisions with current local authorities and qualified professionals. Unverified regional details remain explicitly marked.

Integrated pest management: prevent, monitor, then ask a professional

Pests can spread disease, irritate animals and contaminate feed/milk areas. Start by removing breeding places and blocking access. Identify the pest before choosing a control. The checklist is environmental and physical; it does not select a pesticide.

Mosquitoes

Remove standing water where safe, fix leaks, keep drains flowing, screen clean water storage, and avoid creating pools while cooling animals. Do not larvicide drinking water or ponds without a product expressly authorized for that use and competent local advice.

Flies

Regular manure/urine removal, dry resting areas, spill cleanup, ventilation and protected feed/storage lower breeding and attraction. Monitor fly pressure and animal skin irritation; contact the veterinarian if lesions, anemia, fever or weakness occur.

Ticks

Inspect animals during routine safe handling, recording date, animal ID, body location and burden. Photograph only if safe. Quarantine incoming animals and ask the veterinarian whether examination/testing or a control product is appropriate. Tick-borne disease symptoms need veterinary assessment.

Rodents and night precautions

Close feed bins; clear clutter; repair gaps; manage spilled feed; use secured traps inaccessible to animals; inspect wiring and water lines for gnawing. At night, keep ventilation open, secure gates, use safe lighting and perform a quiet visual animal/water/security check. Do not burn leaves, smoke, or use open flames to repel insects inside a shed.

Why it matters

Use only locally approved products with veterinary direction and label instructions. Observe milk/meat withdrawal requirements and protect workers from chemical exposure. Do not mix chemicals, spray animals or feed areas contrary to labels, or apply pesticide where milk can be contaminated.

Practical guidance

For ticks or skin lesions, note distribution, dates, animal IDs and photos when safe; seek veterinary identification because not every skin problem is the same. Keep equipment and needles from being shared between animals unless cleaned/handled under professional protocol.

Use this safely

Incoming animal biosecurity: pre-purchase health exam; separate arrival area; dedicated tools and clothing; monitor appetite, temperature if trained, dung, breathing, skin and milk; test or extend isolation only as advised by veterinarian.
