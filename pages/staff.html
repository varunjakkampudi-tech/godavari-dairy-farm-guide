Guide · evidence-aware planning

Employee and farm staffing plan

One worker may complete a small herd routine, but one person cannot provide reliable seven-day leave cover or be available for continuous emergencies. Budget backup labor and fair, safe working arrangements.

Evidence and safety: General guidance only. Confirm plot, buyer, legal and veterinary decisions with current local authorities and qualified professionals. Unverified regional details remain explicitly marked.

Why it matters

Write a job description with animal observation, feed/water, cleaning, hygienic milking, milk measurement, records, equipment care and escalation. Train using demonstrations and observed practice. Do not assign diagnosis, injections, IV fluids, delivery assistance or independent medicine selection.

Practical guidance

Define shift boundaries, weekly rest, leave, accommodation (if any), meals, protective equipment, wage terms, accident response, access to emergency numbers and who covers the herd when the employee is absent. Verify applicable wage and labor rules with the Labour Department or qualified adviser.

Use this safely

Control milk reconciliation with animal-wise yields, collection slip comparison, quantity and quality results, rejected/withheld milk entries and settlement records. Keep cash handling separate and retain receipts. Use owner review and a second contact for discrepancies.

Local application

Plan two trained relief contacts before buying stock. If the employee quits, pause purchases/expansion, arrange competent temporary care, communicate with the veterinarian and buyer, and do not leave animals without daily care.
