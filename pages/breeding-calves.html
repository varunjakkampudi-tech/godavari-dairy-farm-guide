Breeding, pregnancy and calf care

Record every observed heat, service/insemination, sire/semen ID if available, pregnancy diagnosis, expected calving window, calving date, calf identity, placenta status, treatments and milk withdrawal. Ask a veterinarian/AI technician to determine breeding timing and pregnancy status. Do not rely on a fixed calendar alone.

Evidence and safety: General guidance only. Confirm plot, buyer, legal and veterinary decisions with current local authorities and qualified professionals. Unverified regional details remain explicitly marked.

Why it matters

Prepare a clean, dry, quiet calving area and save the veterinarian’s number. Observe without unnecessary disturbance. Call immediately for prolonged hard straining without progress, abnormal presentation, severe distress, heavy bleeding, prolapse, or a buffalo unable to stand. Do not pull a calf unless a trained professional directs/assists.

Practical guidance

After birth, ensure the calf is breathing and gets timely adequate colostrum under veterinary guidance; dip/navel care only with an approved protocol; identify and weigh/estimate weight; monitor suckling, warmth, hydration and dung. Calf diarrhea, weakness, not suckling or breathing difficulty requires urgent veterinary advice.

Use this safely

Keep calves in clean, dry, ventilated housing with age-appropriate feed and water access. Separate sick calves, clean feeding utensils and track growth and health. Vaccination, deworming and disbudding/castration decisions belong to the local veterinarian and applicable rules.
