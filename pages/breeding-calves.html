Breeding, pregnancy and calf care

Record every observed heat, service/insemination, sire/semen ID if available, pregnancy diagnosis, expected calving window, calving date, calf identity, placenta status, treatments and milk withdrawal. Ask a veterinarian/AI technician to determine breeding timing and pregnancy status. Do not rely on a fixed calendar alone.

Evidence and safety: General guidance only. Confirm plot, buyer, legal and veterinary decisions with current local authorities and qualified professionals. Unverified regional details remain explicitly marked.

Breeding-to-calf record timeline

This is a planning and observation checklist, not a pregnancy calculator or veterinary protocol. Have the veterinarian/AI technician set the animal-specific breeding, pregnancy-check and expected-calving plan. Dates can shift; do not treat an estimated date as a guaranteed delivery date.

  1. Before service

    Confirm the animal ID and health history. Record the last calving, observed heats, previous services, medicines and reproductive events. Ask the veterinarian/technician whether the animal is ready and which service plan applies.

  2. Heat or service observed

    Record date, time, signs noticed, service method and technician/sire or semen ID when supplied. Do not give hormones or repeat a service based on this guide; ask the technician about timing and next steps.

  3. Pregnancy follow-up

    Record the professional’s pregnancy-check result, date and instructions. Ask when to recheck and how to document a confirmed or uncertain result. Do not infer pregnancy from appearance or missed heat alone.

  4. During pregnancy

    Keep dated health, feed and body-condition notes. Discuss ration changes, workload, vaccines, parasite plans and any medicine with the veterinarian, including pregnancy safety and milk/meat withdrawal.

  5. Prepare for the expected calving window

    Use the professional’s estimated window. Prepare a clean, dry, quiet area, fresh water, safe lighting, observation/relief-worker plan, phone access and veterinary contact. Avoid sudden unreviewed feed or medicine changes.

  6. Calving and urgent signs

    Observe calmly from a safe distance and note when signs start. Call the veterinarian promptly for prolonged hard straining without progress, abnormal presentation, severe distress, heavy bleeding, prolapse, or a dam/calf that is weak or unable to stand. Do not pull, insert a hand, or use instruments unless a veterinarian is physically directing a trained person.

  7. Newborn and follow-up

    Record birth time, calf identity/sex, dam ID, apparent vigor, suckling and colostrum plan, and professional advice. Keep the calf clean and dry. Weakness, failure to suckle, breathing difficulty or diarrhea needs prompt veterinary help; do not force-feed an unresponsive calf. Use only a veterinarian-approved navel and feeding protocol.

Why it matters

Prepare a clean, dry, quiet calving area and save the veterinarian’s number. Observe without unnecessary disturbance. Call immediately for prolonged hard straining without progress, abnormal presentation, severe distress, heavy bleeding, prolapse, or a buffalo unable to stand. Do not pull a calf unless a trained professional directs/assists.

Practical guidance

After birth, ensure the calf is breathing and gets timely adequate colostrum under veterinary guidance; dip/navel care only with an approved protocol; identify and weigh/estimate weight; monitor suckling, warmth, hydration and dung. Calf diarrhea, weakness, not suckling or breathing difficulty requires urgent veterinary advice.

Use this safely

Keep calves in clean, dry, ventilated housing with age-appropriate feed and water access. Separate sick calves, clean feeding utensils and track growth and health. Vaccination, deworming and disbudding/castration decisions belong to the local veterinarian and applicable rules.
