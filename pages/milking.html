Hygienic milking standard procedure

Prepare a clean, dry milking location and clean food-grade containers. Keep sick, treated or abnormal milk separate according to veterinarian and buyer instructions. Wash hands and inspect the buffalo before milking.

Evidence and safety: General guidance only. Confirm plot, buyer, legal and veterinary decisions with current local authorities and qualified professionals. Unverified regional details remain explicitly marked.

Detailed step-by-step procedure
  1. Wash hands; wear clean clothing and cover cuts.
  2. Prepare clean food-grade containers and functional equipment.
  3. Observe the animal’s behavior, appetite, gait, udder and general condition.
  4. Keep treated animals and milk under a separate, clearly identified withdrawal plan.
  5. Use a calm, consistent routine and a safe, clean, non-slip place.
  6. Inspect udder and teats; note pain, heat, swelling, wounds or skin changes.
  7. Clean visibly soiled teats with a safe approved method; avoid contaminating clean milk.
  8. Use one clean towel per animal or single-use towel; never share a dirty cloth.
  9. Dry teats completely before milking.
  10. Check foremilk in a suitable strip cup/surface; do not mix abnormal milk.
  11. Milk gently and completely using trained hand technique or correctly serviced machine.
  12. Stop and seek advice if pain, injury, abnormal milk or distress occurs.
  13. Apply suitable post-milking teat care according to label and veterinary direction.
  14. Measure by animal and milking using a clean, calibrated scale or marked vessel.
  15. Filter through clean food-grade material; keep covered from dust and insects.
  16. Cool or hand off promptly under verified buyer, equipment and food-safety instructions.
  17. Record yield, milk quality/rejection and any withheld volume by animal.
  18. Follow buyer acceptance criteria; never blend abnormal or withdrawal milk to conceal it.
  19. Clean and sanitize equipment using manufacturer directions and approved products.
  20. Dry/store equipment protected; record abnormalities and veterinary follow-up.

Stop and escalate: Clots, flakes, blood, watery milk, udder pain/swelling, fever, sudden yield drop or animal distress require separation of milk and veterinary/buyer guidance. These signs do not diagnose mastitis by themselves. Never return treated milk to sale until the prescribed withdrawal period and buyer acceptance are confirmed.

Why it matters

Clean visibly soiled teats with safe clean water or an approved method, then dry each teat with an individual clean disposable towel or designated clean towel. Check foremilk into a strip cup or appropriate surface for clots, flakes, watery appearance or blood. Do not mix abnormal milk with sale milk.

Practical guidance

Milk with clean hands or sanitized machine equipment, consistently and gently. Avoid wet-hand milking, pulling, floor contamination or using a shared dirty cloth. Apply a suitable post-milking teat disinfectant according to its label and veterinary advice.

Use this safely

Filter using a clean food-grade filter; measure and record milk by animal and milking; protect from dust, insects and heat. Deliver promptly to the buyer’s validated collection/cooling process. If chilling is used, follow applicable dairy guidance and equipment instructions; no universal safe delay is claimed here.

Local application

After milking, disassemble and clean equipment using manufacturer-recommended detergent, water temperature and sanitation steps. Dry and store protected. Follow veterinary treatment withdrawal periods and the buyer’s rejection rules exactly.
