Proposed business plan · November 2026 · West Godavari

₹15 lakh: continuously rotating buffalo dairy

Goal: avoid all animals drying off together, maintain saleable milk through staggered calvings, raise selected calves and maintain liquidity. This is a conditional business model, not a guarantee of positive daily profit or a validated local investment case.

Decision: do NOT buy ten 10+ litre Murrah buffaloes immediately just to meet a headcount. At ₹15 lakh, start with at most eight productive adults under the assumptions below, phase purchases, retain at least ₹3 lakh liquidity, and buy only after independently verified milk production and buyer terms. A 10+ L/day seller demonstration is not a 10 L/day lactation average.

1 · Biological constraints (not interchangeable)

  • ICAR-CIRB reports Murrah lactation length 290–310 days; calving interval 14.1–19.9 months; 305-day milk yield 1,003–2,057 kg in its breed summary, with individual variation. Its Murrah profile describes 300-day lactation and 453-day calving interval. The latter implies roughly 66% of adults lactating on average if cycles actually achieve that interval, not 100%.
  • With an illustrative 300-day lactation and 453-day calving interval, dry/nonmilking days total about 153 per cycle, not necessarily only the final 60 days before calving. Veterinary breeding management determines dates; do not assume every conception or calving occurs on schedule.
  • Buffalo gestation is approximately 305–330 days. Pregnancy must be professionally confirmed, and expected calving is a window, not a guaranteed day.
  • Female calves are not ready to milk in 1–2 years. ICAR-CIRB lists Murrah age at first calving about 40–54 months. A growing heifer ties up cash and space for years; selling a calf is not assured profit.

Primary sources: ICAR-CIRB breed data · ICAR Murrah profile · ICAR reproduction · NDDB calf rearing.

3 · Staggered calving plan for 8 adults

Use individual animal IDs and target future calving slots spaced roughly every two months. Purchase only after verifying each animal's actual previous calving date, pregnancy and forecast due date. Expected herd math: 8 × 300 / 453 ≈ 5.3 animals lactating on an average day over repeated successful cycles; 5 or 6 is a better planning center than claiming 8 always milking. Illness, delayed conception and real calving clusters can push it below five. At 15-month cycles, eight animals imply approximately one calving every 1.9 months on average. Calendars drift: replan monthly, don't rely on fixed two-month sales events.

Purchase gates: Months 0–1: purchase up to four healthy lactating animals from different calving cohorts only after buyer contract, shed and reserve are ready. Months 2–3: add up to two with complementary vet-confirmed stages and healthy 2-day observed milk test. Months 4–6: add final up to two only after actual first-quarter collections cover variable costs and minimum reserve remains. A newly purchased dry pregnant animal has no milk sales until calving; weigh transition disease and price risk. Never buy an animal merely to hit the calendar.

Monthly control: Plot next 18 months of actual last calving, service, pregnancy test, expected calving window, likely dry-off, milk trend. If projected saleable volume dips below buyer break-even requirement, postpone expansion, maintain extra cash and arrange a backup milk supply agreement if buyer commitments require it; do not force breeding or sell healthy animals solely because they are dry.

5 · Calves are a separate long-term stock ledger

  • Record birth/sex, ID, dam, health and feed cost for every calf. Keep immediate colostrum and neonatal care under veterinary guidance, not a DIY treatment plan.
  • Healthy female calves may be retained only if future replacement value exceeds several years of rearing, space, labor, breeding risk and delayed cash return. Female buffaloes often first calve well beyond two years.
  • Male calves and surplus heifers may be sold legally at documented local market prices when appropriate; never budget hypothetical calf sale values as guaranteed monthly income.
  • Cap retained heifers based on feed/space and reserve; budget recurring calf feed separately. At one calf per successful adult calving, a well-cycling eight-adult herd might produce roughly 6 births per year before fertility losses, stillbirths, and deaths—not eight sellable calves every year.
  • Replacement: review actual low yield, repeat breeding failure, chronic disease and welfare with veterinarian. Replace only when lifetime economics justify, with quarantine and sale/transfer records; never automatically cull at first dry-off.

6 · Operating rules and 90-day evidence gates

  1. Before purchase: obtain dated buyer procurement terms (fat/SNF and rejection deductions), an actual payment sample, 3 independent animal quotes, feed suppliers' delivered prices, vet and AI technician availability, lease/water/flood evaluation.
  2. At purchase: independently identify animal, parity, actual last calving, health and pregnancy check; observe two full morning/evening milking rounds, check adulteration/mastitis and body condition with a veterinarian; insure, quarantine and record transport/ownership.
  3. Daily: record each buffalo's morning+evening yield, fed quantities, refusals, illnesses, drugs/withdrawal, cash collected and spent. Reject mixing abnormal/withdrawal milk into saleable supply.
  4. Weekly: review milk per lactating adult and cost per saleable litre, feed stocks, buyer settlement, water/cooling, calf growth and staff relief.
  5. Monthly: veterinarian-guided breeding checks and refreshed 18-month calving forecast; run low/base/high cash scenarios in annual finance calculator and finance studio; reconcile reserve and projected 90-day trough.
  6. Expansion only after: 90 days real records, buyer payments received, all fixed costs included, and conservative forward case positive while keeping reserve. If not, pause at 4–6 rather than expanding to eight.
