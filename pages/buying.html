Buying dairy buffaloes safely

Buy animals one at a time or in staggered groups so the herd does not all enter dry period together. A practical plan is to first confirm buyers and facilities, then purchase two healthy animals at different lactation stages and add the next only after routines and cash flow stabilize. The exact sequence depends on supply, calving records and veterinary advice.

Why it matters

Observe the animal in its home setting. Match tag and documents; ask exact calving date, lactation number, breeding dates, previous disease, mastitis history, yield records, medication and withdrawal periods. Observe multiple complete milkings over more than one day when possible; record start/end, quantity and milking behavior.

Practical guidance

Pre-purchase examination checklist: body condition and gait; eyes, breathing and appetite; udder symmetry, skin and teat condition; foremilk appearance; veterinarian-selected mastitis screening; feet/hooves; teeth as an approximate age clue; pregnancy/reproductive examination by a veterinarian; vaccination/deworming records; identification; transport fitness; and purchase agreement.

Use this safely

Warning signs include a seller preventing independent examination or milking observation, unexplained high yield claims, mismatched animal IDs, no calving history, hidden udder quarters, recent unexplained treatment, pressure to pay immediately, or an animal that cannot walk comfortably. Do not pay a premium for unsupported claims.

Local application

Use a written bill of sale stating identity, agreed price, disclosed health/reproductive status, seller/buyer details, date and remedies for misrepresentation. Arrange transport under applicable rules, insurance if available, and veterinary-guided isolation before mixing with resident animals.
