# Hybrid milk + calf (దూడ) business operating plan — West Godavari

**Version:** 2026-11-13 | **Status:** decision-support proposal; **NOT a verified profit forecast or approval to invest**.  
**Target geography:** Tanuku–Peravali–Bhimavaram, Andhra Pradesh.  
**Business streams:** (1) buffalo/cow milk to contracted shops and dairy collection centers; (2) selectively raised calves (దూడలు) for lawful sale or breeding-stock replacement; (3) manure only if a confirmed paying buyer exists.

> **Reality check:** There is no guaranteed zero-loss dairy. A farm is not profitable merely because the animals produce milk daily. Annual lactation gaps, calf feed, loan interest, labour, mortality, milk rejection and delayed payments must be funded. Do not buy livestock or build a shed solely from the illustrative figures here.

## 1. Current baseline and recommendation

The owner has considered a **two-Murrah-buffalo pilot**, each offered at approximately **₹1.5 lakh**, maintained at an existing partner farm with four other buffaloes; later a **leased one-/two-acre farm** and a possible **10-animal unit**. The earlier expectation of **13 L per animal per day** and prices of **₹60/L or ₹70–₹80/L** are **owner-reported assumptions, not verified yields or buyer rates**. The intended ₹3 lakh pilot purchase allocation leaves **no room for transport, insurance, examination, dry-period feed or emergencies**.

**Recommended:** phase-gated model. First validate *one* buyer settlement and actual 14–30 day yield plus fat/SNF for every proposed animal, veterinary examination, and partner economics. Start with **2 animals only if the partner can absorb fixed staff and land costs and the cash reserve is separately funded**. Do not jump straight to ten. If the two-animal plan is cash-negative at verified values, defer purchase or renegotiate the partner deal. Add animals in spaced cohorts **only after profitable realized settlements and sufficient feed, water and labour capacity**.

Keep the milk enterprise and calf enterprise as **separate cost centers**; calves are not guaranteed annual cash income.

## 2. Revenue design: no double counting

| Channel | Contract approach | Net-price calculation | Risk control |
|---|---|---|---|
| Dairy society / collection center | Written acceptance, lab-based fat/SNF formula, pickup times, deductions, rejection and settlement date | Actual paid amount / accepted litres (or kg, reconciled consistently) | Default home for milk not sold to shops, if accepted |
| Tea shops, sweet shops, hotels | Standing daily orders; agreed quality, litre/kg unit, sealed food-grade containers, delivery times, invoices and short payment cycle | Sales receipts minus delivery, packing/chilling, rejects, returns, credit losses | Start with 2–3 nearby buyers, weekly settlement; cap credit |
| Direct households (optional) | Prepaid/subscription orders only after legal and cold-chain readiness | Cash collected minus packaging and last-mile expense | Avoid a daily retail route until demand is proven |
| Calf sales | Individual animal record; age, sex, veterinary status, rearing cost, buyer offer and applicable sale/transport rules | Cash received **minus all attributed calf costs and replacement value** | Never count an unsold calf as cash profit |
| Replacement heifers | Retain only those meeting breeding/health/economic criteria | Inventory/asset, **not cash revenue** | Avoid assuming every female calf is worth rearing |
| Manure | Use on fodder first; sell only against signed pickup/order | Receipts less handling/transport | Treat as zero in base case |

**Allocation:** identify the same litre only once: gross milk produced − calf feeding/withheld/abnormal/rejected/spillage = eligible saleable milk; allocate that eligible milk among shops, households and collection center; then deduct **channel-specific** expenses. Never add shop revenue on top of center revenue for the same litres. When shop demand temporarily exceeds farm milk, **do not promise uninterrupted supply** until a legally compliant, quality-tested backup source and buyer permission exist; identify sourced milk separately with traceability and truthful disclosure.

**Practical initial split (not a fixed quota):** offer firm shops only **30–40% of conservatively demonstrated saleable daily output**; reserve the rest for a verified collection center. Raise the shop allocation only after 30 days of delivery performance and net price above the center option. Accept no standing order larger than the **lowest reliably available volume**, not the best lactation-day output.

## 3. Milking and reproductive continuity

**Individual animals, not herd-average promises.** Record date of last calving, daily morning/evening litres, pregnancy diagnosis from vet, planned dry-off (vet-guided), expected calving **window**, and expected return-to-milk window. Milk yield generally declines during lactation; pregnancy does not mean milk output stays constant until calving. Calving and dry periods vary by animal. Never promise 24×7 milk from owned stock.

| Example cohort for a future 10-adult herd | Headcount | Operational purpose |
|---|---:|---|
| A — recently calved, confirmed milking | 3 | Present production; monitor declines |
| B — mid-lactation, different calving dates | 3 | Subsequent months' continuity |
| C — late lactation / confirmed pregnant | 2 | Planned dry-off near vet-advised window |
| D — dry / close to calving | 2 | Feed cost without current milk; prospective future supply |
| **Total** | **10** | **Illustrative mix only, not 8 guaranteed milkers** |

For each purchase, seek a different **verified** calving/pregnancy stage. Do not use asserted pregnancy or due date without professional examination. Plan a staggered **2–3 month purchase/calving window** only when the actual animals allow it; artificial insemination and reproductive interventions must be directed by a qualified veterinarian/technician. Target **minimum daily contractual supply** on the basis of a separately simulated **worst month**, not the 8 milking animals assumed in this illustrative cohort. Keep a backup procurement contract or reduce standing shop orders if the worst-month owned supply cannot meet them.

## 4. Calf business — economics before breeding-stock speculation

1. **At birth:** identify calf and dam, record date/sex, observe colostrum intake and wellbeing, obtain veterinarian-approved colostrum/feeding, navel, vaccination/deworming and disease-control instructions. Do not sell, transport or separate calves in ways inconsistent with welfare and applicable law.
2. **Monthly:** track separately milk consumed or milk-replacer cost, starter/feed/fodder, housing, labour, medicines/vet, transport, insurance, mortality and opportunity cost. Milk fed to calves is deducted from milk saleable volume.
3. **Choice at each decision gate:** retain a promising female as **replacement** only if genetics, health, space and lifetime rearing economics justify; otherwise seek a lawful, welfare-compliant buyer. Do not presume high resale value or demand for male calves. Avoid holding every calf for 1–2 years without a buyer and a feed budget.
4. **Sale:** inspect buyer credentials and local livestock sale/transport requirements; issue receipt, transfer identifiers and health records. Keep buyer, payment, animal movement and welfare records. Do not treat projected sale proceeds as monthly working capital.
5. **Calf contribution formula:** sale price − (milk foregone + calf feed + health + allocated housing/labour + transport + transaction expense + losses) = realized calf contribution. A retained heifer is replacement stock, *not sale profit*.

**Go/no-go for rearing:** (verified expected sale price × conservatively estimated sale probability) must exceed remaining costs **and** justify risk/space; if not, choose a lawful earlier sale/placement subject to vet/welfare rules. Actual sale opportunities in West Godavari are **NOT VERIFIED**.

## 5. Illustrative annual model — NOT local quotations

All values are deliberately editable placeholders; compare with 12 months of receipts and individual records before spending. Assume equal milk yield during each lactating day as a **simplification** (real lactation curves vary). Owner labour, income tax, interest, depreciation, major replacement purchases and death loss are **NOT included** in the operating surplus below; calculate them separately before claiming net profit.

| Parameter | Partner pilot: 2 adults | Expansion: 10 adults |
|---|---:|---:|
| Milk yield per lactating animal-day | 7 L | 8 L |
| Lactating days per animal per year | 260 | 270 |
| Saleable share after calf use/withhold/reject | 92% | 94% |
| **Blended realized price**, after channel deductions | ₹60/L | ₹62/L |
| Annual milk receipts | **₹200,928** | **₹1,258,848** |
| Feed/fodder per head per day (365 days) | ₹160 | ₹175 |
| Annual feed/fodder | ₹116,800 | ₹638,750 |
| Partner servicing / staff | ₹72,000 | ₹216,000 |
| Land lease | Included in partner fee | ₹72,000 |
| Vet, utilities, handling, insurance, other | ₹20,000 | ₹190,000 |
| **Annual cash operating surplus / (shortfall)** | **(₹7,872)** | **₹142,098** |
| Monthly equivalent (not monthly cash forecast) | **(₹656)** | **₹11,842** |

These cases are *not apples-to-apples labour contracts*; the pilot assumes a negotiated partner servicing fee (₹6,000/month), whereas 10 adults need their own staff/land. Calf rearing expenses beyond the embedded saleable-milk reduction must be added if calves are retained. The ten-head case has only a narrow operating cushion relative to capital invested; **₹142,098 is not take-home profit**.

**10-adult threshold at these modeled costs:** saleable annual volume = **20,304 L**; cash operating break-even realized blended milk price ≈ **₹55.00/L**, *before* debt service, depreciation, owner's unpaid work and capital recovery. To afford those items, the required price is higher. Price can vary by fat/SNF and buyer; ₹62/L is **not a verified quote**.

**Downside case:** 10 × 6.5 L × 240 milk days × 90% saleable × ₹52/L = **₹730,080 gross milk receipts**, far below the illustrated ₹1,116,750 annual operating cost. Loss ≈ **₹386,670 before additional stress costs**. Calf sale is zero in base/downside until cash is collected.

**Cash safety rule:** maintain at least **six months of committed cash operating obligations**, including feed, labour, lease and debt service, in a separate accessible reserve; for the ten-head illustrative non-debt operating costs alone this is about **₹558,375**. Add capex, loan EMI, replacement reserves, animal insurance deductibles and transport separately. A reserve limits damage; it does not make a bad business profitable.

**Scenario worksheet equations:**
- Milk sold/year = sum by animal of (daily lactation litres × actual lactation days) minus all withheld/calf/household/spill/rejected litres.
- Receipts = Σ(channel litres × channel realized paid price) **minus** refunds, bad debt and related selling costs, unless already netted.
- Operating cash flow = collected receipts − actual paid operating costs; monthly flows matter more than annual average.
- True annual economic profit = operating cash flow − interest − annualized capital consumption/depreciation − replacement provisions − owner's paid/valued labour ± changes in inventory; distinguish loan *principal* in cash-flow rather than counting it as an accounting expense.
- Shop minimum profitable price = collection-center net alternative + *incremental* delivery/packing/chilling/credit cost per litre + margin for operational risk.
- Break-even litres = fixed annual cash costs / (realized price per litre − marginal variable cost per litre), **only where denominator is positive**; validate costs that scale with headcount separately.

## 6. Feed, husbandry and asset-risk controls

Use individual, nutritionist/vet-balanced rations by weight, milk yield, body condition, life stage, feed quality and season. **Do not prescribe a fixed feed kg/day for all animals.** Budget and record green/dry fodder, concentrates, minerals, water, testing and seasonal shortages. Test whether one/two leased acres actually supply enough fodder *in each month*, not just annually; contract dry fodder and maintain safe inventory. Compare fodder growing plus irrigation/labour/land cost to purchasing. NDDB and ICAR advisory material explains how nutritional needs rise with yield; do not starve dry or pregnant animals to preserve cash.

Arrange reliable drinking water, shaded ventilated non-slip shed, drainage, manure handling, flood-safe access, separate calf area and quarantine/isolation facilities. Adopt animal-level IDs, veterinary preventive plan, disease reporting, routine welfare checks, insurance quotations with policy exclusions, and clean milking workflow. Follow veterinary drug milk withdrawal restrictions; never blend or sell abnormal or withdrawal milk. Do not buy a high-yield buffalo based only on seller claims: observe multiple milkings, confirm health, pregnancy/lactation, age, prior records, udder health, fat/SNF and transport fitness independently.

## 7. Daily shop-delivery and collections SOP

**Before purchase:** get at least one **written, dated** buyer rate/settlement and one backup milk off-take arrangement. For shops, obtain actual order quantity, location, payment cycle and any return policy. Verify legal classification and cold-chain requirements before sales.

**Every morning/evening:** record each animal’s milk and withheld quantity → inspect/segregate abnormalities → use clean food-grade equipment → measure and trace each batch → promptly cool or deliver through a validated, temperature-controlled process → record volume and destination → obtain signed/dated delivery acknowledgement → reconcile evening stock, rejection and payments. Route surplus only to a center that actually accepts it at that time. No milk should be sold after uncontrolled heat exposure or a failed safety check.

**Weekly:** reconcile liters produced vs calf use vs shop sold vs center sold vs withheld/rejected/spoiled; reconcile cash/UPI/bank collections vs invoices and customer arrears; check feed consumed vs recorded yield, worker cover, animals' vet notes, and customer churn. Escalate credit overdue above agreed limit; stop additional supply rather than financing shops indefinitely.

**Monthly:** by-animal milk margin, price-by-channel net of selling costs, cost per saleable litre, cash burn, debt service coverage, animal pregnancy/dry/health calendar, calf inventory and rearing cost, fodder sufficiency and sensitivity scenario. Expansion is prohibited if an unresolved adverse trend or insufficient cash reserve exists.

## 8. Compliance and milk-quality gate

FSSAI's **11 March 2026 advisory** says milk producers other than specified dairy cooperative members selling their entire milk to the cooperative, and milk vendors, must obtain the applicable FSSAI registration/licence. Applicable tier depends on actual business category, annual turnover, milk handling and activities; direct shop/household sales and third-party milk trading need classification checks. Verify via official FoSCoS and district food-safety office before launch. The previous simple ₹12 lakh threshold alone is *not a complete classification rule*. Do not claim packaging, chilling, pasteurization, curd or other processing is permitted under a registration intended for a different activity. Validate labeling, transport, temperature, hygiene and sampling obligations for the final business model. Keep milk sourced from other farms truthful, documented and quality-tested.

Applicable calf ownership, identification, livestock-market, animal-welfare and transport requirements must be checked with the local Animal Husbandry office and other competent authorities. This document makes **no blanket legal assurance** for calf sale routes.

## 9. Stage gates (owner sign-off required)

**Gate 0 — before paying deposit:** 3 written center/shop buyer quotes; actual milk fat/SNF settlement formula; 2 local feed quotes; veterinarian and animal purchase inspection agreement; costed partner contract (who pays feed, medicines, loss, dry periods, calves, insurance, vet, transport); cold-chain compliance; legal category; cash reserves. **Stop** if buyer or animal data cannot be verified.

**Gate 1 — pilot 30–90 days:** independently verified 2-animal yields and saleable liters; 30+ days collected cash, a known dry-period obligation, rejection/delivery tracking, partner and insurance terms. Do not treat one good month as annual profitability. **Stop buying** if the pilot loses cash under conservative projections.

**Gate 2 — owned 4–6 animals:** written lease with livestock, shed, irrigation and waste permissions; flood/groundwater/delivery feasibility; separate hired worker relief arrangement; monthly fodder plan; backup collection. Build only proportionately and with quotes.

**Gate 3 — 8–10 adults:** require an animal-specific 12-month schedule, **worst-month** contracted milk cover, emergency buffer, 12-month finance studio model with loan/capex, at least three months of actual operating evidence, and all professional approvals. No automatic scale-up.

**Hard-stop triggers:** no legal sale pathway; unsafe milk or withdrawal violations; no drinking water; absent veterinarian/relief labour; no reserve for next month's essential bills; feed deficit without a secured purchase; persistent negative contribution after realistic fixed costs; unverified animal health; substantial customer payment defaults.
